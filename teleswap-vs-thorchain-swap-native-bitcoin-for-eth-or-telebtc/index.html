TeleSwap vs THORChain: Swap Native Bitcoin for ETH or teleBTC

TeleSwap suits a Bitcoin holder who wants BTC exposure as teleBTC on a supported EVM chain; THORChain suits one who wants native ETH out. For the bridge route, use TeleSwap to bridge or swap Bitcoin across blockchains. THORChain trades through liquidity pools, while the bridge verifies a Bitcoin deposit and mints teleBTC against BTC held by collateralized Lockers.

How Do TeleSwap and THORChain Handle a Native BTC Deposit?

THORChain turns a native BTC deposit into a native destination asset through RUNE-paired pools; TeleSwap turns a verified BTC deposit into teleBTC that can be used or swapped on an EVM chain. Both start with a Bitcoin transaction, but the asset delivered at the end determines which route fits your goal.

On THORChain, you send BTC to a network vault with a transaction memo specifying the output asset and destination address. On Bitcoin, the instruction can be carried in OP_RETURN data. Nodes observe the deposit and execute the swap. For BTC to ETH, the trade passes through the BTC/RUNE and ETH/RUNE pools before native ETH is sent to your Ethereum address. RUNE is the pool intermediary; you do not need to hold it yourself.

On the bridge route, your Bitcoin transaction sends BTC to a Locker and carries the information needed to identify the destination. TeleportDAO relays Bitcoin block headers to the destination chain, where contracts verify proof of the deposit. The protocol then mints teleBTC against the BTC the Locker holds. A route that requests another supported token can swap the newly minted teleBTC before delivery.

Think of teleBTC as a warehouse receipt: the BTC held by a Locker is the stored asset, and teleBTC is the representation you can use elsewhere. The comparison stops at the receipt. The real mint depends on Bitcoin proof verification, smart contracts and Locker collateral, rather than a clerk issuing paper.

Those mechanics also locate the risks. THORChain relies on node operators that bond RUNE and control network vaults. The bridge relies on overcollateralized Lockers that hold BTC, plus the header relay and contracts that verify deposits. A Locker’s available capacity matters because it must be able to take the BTC deposit; pool depth matters on THORChain because the trade moves the pool price.

What Do You Get When You Swap 0.5 BTC?

With a 0.5 BTC deposit, THORChain can deliver native ETH, while the bridge route can deliver teleBTC or a supported token swapped from it. Picture two holders starting with the same Bitcoin wallet balance but planning to use different assets afterward.

Say the first holder wants ETH in an Ethereum wallet. The 0.5 BTC enters THORChain’s BTC pool, is exchanged through RUNE, and exits the ETH pool as native ETH. The amount received depends partly on the depth of both pools relative to the trade. A larger trade against shallow liquidity moves the price further and incurs a larger slip-based liquidity fee. Bitcoin transaction cost and the outbound Ethereum fee also affect the final amount.

For a trade of that size, compare a regular swap with a streaming swap. Streaming splits the trade into smaller swaps executed across blocks. Each smaller swap moves the pools less, and arbitrage can rebalance them between executions. That can reduce the total slippage fee and increase the ETH received, at the cost of waiting longer for the trade to finish. The fastest quote is therefore often not the cheapest one. Compare the quoted ETH after fees and the expected completion time, rather than choosing by speed alone.

Say the second holder wants to use BTC exposure in EVM DeFi. Their 0.5 BTC goes to a Locker. After the relay supplies Bitcoin headers and the deposit is verified on-chain, teleBTC is minted for the destination route. If the holder requested another supported token, the route can swap from teleBTC into that output. The amount available depends on the route’s applicable fees and whether a Locker has capacity for the deposit; a token swap adds its own execution cost.

Which Route Fits What You Want to Do Next?

THORChain fits a holder who intends to spend, transfer or hold the destination chain’s native coin. Native ETH arrives as ETH on Ethereum, so there is no BTC-backed token to manage afterward. For the 0.5 BTC holder whose goal is ETH, the useful comparison is how much ETH the chosen swap delivers and when it arrives.

The bridge fits a holder who wants a BTC-linked asset available to an EVM application. Before choosing a DeFi venue, confirm that it accepts teleBTC: a contract that accepts one BTC token may not accept another. At teleswap.net, you can bridge and swap Bitcoin and other assets across blockchains. If you instead choose a swapped output token, assess that token as the asset you will actually hold.

Related Questions

Is teleBTC the Same as WBTC?

teleBTC and WBTC are distinct BTC-representing tokens with different contracts and backing arrangements. teleBTC is minted from a verified Bitcoin deposit held by collateralized Lockers; WBTC uses its own issuance and custody system. Their symbols and contract addresses visibly differ. Treating them as interchangeable can lead you to choose a DeFi market that will not accept the token you receive.

Which Chains Can THORChain Send Native BTC To?

THORChain can send native BTC to a Bitcoin address. It can take native BTC as input and send a different supported asset to that asset’s own chain, such as native ETH to Ethereum. Sending a Bitcoin payout to an Ethereum address confuses the input asset with the output chain.

Can TeleSwap Swap BTC Directly Into Another Token?

Yes, a supported route can take native BTC and deliver another token. The bridge first verifies the Bitcoin deposit and mints teleBTC; the token swap follows from that bridge leg. If your intended output is native ETH, compare that route’s output with a THORChain BTC-to-ETH swap.

  • Choose THORChain for native ETH or another supported native asset out; compare pool depth and streaming swap quotes.
  • Choose the bridge route for teleBTC on a supported EVM chain; confirm your intended application accepts it.
  • For either route, judge the 0.5 BTC trade by the asset and amount you receive, not just the asset you send.
