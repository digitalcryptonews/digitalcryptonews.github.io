How to Claim a Polygon PoS Withdrawal on Ethereum

How to claim a Polygon bridge withdrawal on Ethereum: wait for the burn checkpoint, submit the exit claim in Polygon Portal, and receive tokens in your wallet.

The Finality DeskNov 19, 2026 · 21:19 UTC5 min read#d20818

Claim a Polygon bridge withdrawal on Ethereum after Polygon checkpoints the burn: switch to Ethereum in Polygon Portal and confirm the exit transaction to release the tokens.

  • The burn starts the withdrawal on Polygon; the claim releases the tokens on Ethereum.
  • The claim needs the checkpoint proof, so submitting before the burn is checkpointed will fail.
  • Anyone can submit the proof, but the tokens go to the address that burned them.

The checkpoint makes the Ethereum claim valid

A Polygon PoS withdrawal has two separate onchain events: the burn on Polygon and the release on Ethereum. The burn removes the bridged tokens from Polygon. It does not, by itself, put them in your Ethereum wallet.

Polygon validators periodically submit checkpoints to Ethereum. A checkpoint commits to a range of Polygon blocks, allowing Ethereum to verify that the block containing your burn belongs to Polygon’s history. The exit proof carries the burn receipt and its inclusion proof. RootChainManager checks that proof when its exit function is called, then the bridge releases the corresponding asset on Ethereum. Polygon’s RootChainManager contract source shows the exit function and checkpoint membership verification.

Before the checkpoint includes your burn, there is no valid proof for the Ethereum contract to accept. An early claim attempt fails; it does not complete the withdrawal. Wait for Polygon Portal’s transaction status to change to “ready to claim” before submitting the exit.

A Polygon cross-chain bridge withdrawal can also be completed by a wallet other than the one that initiated the burn. The exit proof is about the burn and its destination, not who pays for the Ethereum transaction. The released tokens still go to the address that burned them, while the wallet submitting the claim pays Ethereum gas in ETH.

That distinction is useful when the withdrawing wallet has no ETH. Keep that wallet’s address as the destination, but have another wallet with ETH submit the claim. The second wallet pays the transaction cost; it does not receive the withdrawn tokens. If you started a bridge to Polygon transfer in reverse and are now completing its exit, this is the Ethereum-side action that finishes it. The Polygon bridge is relevant here because it gives you a way to bridge assets to Polygon; for this withdrawal, the remaining task is to claim the release on Ethereum.

Claim the withdrawal in Polygon Portal

Start with a Polygon PoS withdrawal that you have already initiated. Have the wallet used for the burn available, plus ETH on Ethereum for the wallet that will submit the claim. Follow these steps in order:

  1. Open Polygon Portal’s transactions list and find the withdrawal you started on Polygon PoS.
  2. Wait until the withdrawal status reads “ready to claim.” This means the burn has been checkpointed and the exit proof can be verified on Ethereum.

    Easy to get wrong: A completed burn is not the same as a claimable withdrawal. Trying to claim before the checkpoint arrives makes the Ethereum transaction fail, so wait for the ready status rather than resubmitting early.

  3. Switch the wallet network to Ethereum. Use the wallet that will pay the Ethereum gas; it can be a different address from the one that burned the tokens.
  4. Click “claim” for the withdrawal and confirm the transaction in your wallet. This submits the exit proof to RootChainManager on Ethereum. The claim transaction’s gas is paid in ETH.
  5. Wait for the Ethereum transaction to confirm, then check the token balance in the Ethereum wallet that received the withdrawal. The balance increasing is the sign that the claim worked.

If another wallet paid the claim gas, check the burn address for the incoming tokens, not the gas-paying address. The burn determines where the bridge releases the tokens.

Use the received Ethereum balance to confirm completion

The withdrawal is complete when Ethereum confirms the claim and the token balance at the burn address increases. A confirmed Polygon burn alone is not the finish line; the Ethereum claim is the release step. Once the balance has updated, you can use or hold the tokens on Ethereum.

If the withdrawal is still waiting, leave it pending until Portal marks it ready. If it is ready, submit the claim from a wallet with ETH for gas, then verify the balance at the original burn address.

Questions about Polygon PoS withdrawal claims

Why is my Polygon withdrawal not ready to claim?

A Polygon withdrawal is not ready until the block containing its burn is included in an Ethereum checkpoint. Portal cannot submit a valid exit before that checkpoint exists. Wait for the transaction status to update; if it remains pending after the relevant checkpoint, inspect the withdrawal transaction and its status in Portal before trying again.

How much gas does a Polygon exit claim cost?

The claim’s gas cost varies with Ethereum network conditions and the transaction’s execution. The fee is paid in ETH by the wallet that submits the claim. Check the wallet’s quoted fee when you confirm the transaction, and make sure it has enough ETH to cover it.

Can I cancel a Polygon bridge withdrawal?

A Polygon PoS withdrawal cannot be cancelled after the burn has been initiated. The burn starts the exit process, and the remaining step is to claim the tokens on Ethereum after the checkpoint. You can complete that claim later; the key is to wait until it is ready and submit it with a wallet that can pay Ethereum gas.
