EU proposal targets crypto networks in 22nd Russia sanctions round

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions package would add transaction restrictions on 60 banks and target crypto networks, but all 27 EU states must approve it.

The Finality DeskNov 28, 2026 · 01:46 UTC3 min read#6cc686

The European Commission proposed targeting Russian crypto and payment networks as part of its 22nd sanctions package on 23 November, alongside transaction restrictions on about 60 Russian banks. President Ursula von der Leyen’s statement on the proposed package says the crypto measures would also reach third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The package is a proposal; it does not take effect unless approved by all 27 EU member states.

What crypto activity would the proposal restrict?

The Commission’s statement identifies crypto and payment networks as targets, but does not name specific providers, assets, transaction types or technical criteria for applying the restrictions. It says the measures will focus on Russia and include third countries that help Russia evade existing sanctions. The statement also announces a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work and says the EU wants to align enforcement with international partners.

That leaves the proposed crypto perimeter undefined in the public statement. It does not specify whether a particular service would be covered because it processes transfers, provides custody, operates an exchange or facilitates payments. Nor does it describe a blockchain-level restriction. The confirmed development is a proposed sanctions focus on networks and platforms, with implementation details still to be established.

How do the bank restrictions relate to SWIFT?

The proposal would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove those banks from SWIFT, according to von der Leyen. The Associated Press report describes SWIFT as a messaging system used for international payments. Its role is to transmit payment instructions between financial institutions; excluding a bank from that messaging network is distinct from a direct ban on transactions with it.

The Commission is proposing both measures for the banks, but the public statement does not detail their implementation or list the institutions. It also does not say that the crypto restrictions operate through SWIFT. The measures address connected pressure points in Russia’s access to financial services, while using separate descriptions for bank transactions, messaging access and crypto or payment networks.

What else is in the package, and what happens next?

The Commission’s proposal also includes expanding an energy-services ban to electricity services, listing more than 70 additional vessels, and barring tanker vessels insured in Russia. It would prohibit Europeans from holding posts in governing bodies of companies in Russia. For sports and culture, the Commission proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at EU events, banning the display of official Russian symbols, and restricting Russian sponsorship flows to European sports and cultural associations.

Von der Leyen also proposed a visa ban for Russian ex-combatants. These measures, like the financial restrictions, remain subject to approval by all EU member states. AP reported that approval can take weeks and that the proposed measures could change before taking effect. Until the member states agree, the announcement sets out the Commission’s intended scope; it does not establish new obligations for crypto providers or financial institutions.
