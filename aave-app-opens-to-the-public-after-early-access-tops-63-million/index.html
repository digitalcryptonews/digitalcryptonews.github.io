Aave App opens to the public after early access tops $63 million

Aave Labs opened its savings app to the public after early access balances passed $63 million, with deposits routed into Aave lending markets to earn borrower interest.

The Finality DeskNov 26, 2026 · 06:26 UTC3 min read#518a77

Aave Labs opened its Aave App to the public on Nov. 25 after its early access phase drew more than $63 million in user balances in under three months, The Block reported. The app routes deposits into Aave lending markets, where borrower interest becomes saver earnings.

The mechanism is described in Aave Labs’ launch announcement: deposits are supplied to supported Aave markets, and the app allocates them across those markets so the user sees one savings balance. Borrowers must supply collateral worth more than the amount they borrow. That overcollateralization backs loans, while interest paid by borrowers funds deposit earnings. The app abstracts the market allocation and wallet operations from the user; it does not change how lending markets generate yield.

Aave says users can create self-custodial Aave Accounts with an email address or phone number and password, without managing a conventional crypto wallet or recovery phrase. The app is available on iOS and Android. Early access began on iOS on Aug. 26, with Android access following on Nov. 17, according to The Block. The company’s announcement says users in more than 170 countries took part before the public release.

At launch, verified users can receive a variable 6% base rate for 90 days on balances up to $100,000, The Block reported, citing Aave Labs. Eligible users can raise the rate to 7.25% through referrals and recurring deposits. Rates and eligibility vary by market, and Aave says rates are variable rather than guaranteed. Earnings begin when a deposit lands and compound every second. Users can withdraw without lockups or withdrawal penalties.

Bank funding is available to eligible users in the United States, excluding New York, the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area. The Block reported that U.S. connections use licensed third-party providers including Bridge and Astra, while Aave Labs’ Push service provides connections in the UK and EEA. The company says it charges no fees for bank deposits or withdrawals, though a user’s bank may charge its own fee.

Users in supported jurisdictions can also deposit USDC, USDT or GHO from eight networks: Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, BNB Chain, HyperEVM, Monad, Optimism and TRON. Supported stablecoins vary by network. The public launch announcement says Aave plans to add more deposit networks and ways to send and receive money, as well as an Auto-Saver tool, in the coming weeks.

The launch brings a consumer-facing account layer to Aave’s lending markets, but the app’s balance is not a bank deposit. Aave says funds are not insured by a government agency. The Block reported that Aave Labs’ discretionary Balance Protection program may reimburse certain losses under its terms; users do not hold insurance through that program. Returns depend on variable rates and eligible market access, while deposits remain exposed to the risks of the underlying onchain lending system.
