DOJ gets authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance funds

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion tied to Binance’s $6 billion theft, as tracing continues.

The Finality DeskNov 13, 2026 · 17:01 UTC3 min read#062057

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and freeze the remaining funds continue. The Justice Department said the authorization covers part of a larger pool frozen by U.S. agencies and private-sector partners; the Associated Press report also noted that the court action does not itself resolve who will ultimately receive recovered assets.

The distinction matters: the department’s announcement describes court-authorized seizure or restraint, not a completed return of $1.2 billion to Binance or affected users. The government and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total since the theft, according to the Justice Department. More than $1.2 billion of that amount is covered by the court authorization. The release does not specify the assets’ denominations, the court that issued the authorization, or a timetable for forfeiture.

The department says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29, when North Korean military members stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI refers to the actors as “TraderTraitor.” The AP described the incident as a roughly $6.3 billion theft and reported that the FBI had previously attributed it to a North Korean hacking group using that name. The agencies have not publicly detailed the technical entry point or the specific wallets covered by the latest authorization in the announcement.

U.S. investigators are working with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the assets. The department says those joint efforts produced the broader freeze. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued a cyber alert last week asking VASPs to block transactions with or derived from cryptocurrency addresses that TraderTraitor actors use to launder the Binance assets. That request targets service providers able to identify and halt activity involving those addresses; it does not describe a protocol-level reversal of confirmed blockchain transfers.

The tracing and recovery work remains active. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those routes can move funds between networks or services, complicating efforts to follow their path and identify where a provider can intervene. The announcement does not say how much of the more than $2.7 billion frozen total remains accessible, which assets are still moving, or how much of the theft has been recovered into government custody.

Seizure and forfeiture are also separate steps. The department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain funds; it does not announce a final forfeiture judgment. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered property. That process can determine the eventual disposition of assets after they are restrained, but the current announcement does not identify claimants or establish when a decision will be made.

The immediate result is narrower than a full recovery: more than $1.2 billion is now subject to court-authorized seizure or restraint, within a larger freeze exceeding $2.7 billion. Investigators and service providers are still tracing assets across exchanges and cross-chain routes, and the department says the work to freeze, seize and forfeit them is ongoing. Neither the total ultimately recoverable nor the timeline for returning assets has been confirmed.
