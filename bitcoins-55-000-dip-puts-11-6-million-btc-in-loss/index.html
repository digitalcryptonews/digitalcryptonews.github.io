Bitcoin’s $55,000 dip puts 11.6 million BTC in loss

Bitcoin’s weekend slide to about $55,000 pushed more than 11.6 million BTC below their last-move price, while supply in profit fell beneath its FTX-era low.

The Finality DeskNov 25, 2026 · 11:13 UTC3 min read#75cf6e

Bitcoin’s slide to about $55,000 over the weekend pushed more than 11.6 million BTC into loss, a record, according to Glassnode data cited by CoinDesk. The figure counts coins whose last on-chain movement occurred at a higher price than the current market price. It tracks an estimated cost basis across Bitcoin’s unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs), not a tally of holders who have sold at a loss.

Glassnode’s supply-in-loss definition values each Bitcoin UTXO at the price when it was created. If the current price is lower, that output is counted as being in loss. The method uses the last movement as a cost-basis proxy; it does not establish what a holder paid off-chain or whether they intend to sell.

The scale of the move is visible against earlier levels and the related supply-in-profit measure:

  • More than 11.6 million BTC in loss: the record reported after Bitcoin fell to about $55,000. The coins’ last-move prices sit above the current market price, indicating unrealized losses under this measure.
  • About 10.5 million BTC in loss: the level when Bitcoin traded just below $60,000 this summer. CoinDesk said that reading was comparable with major market lows in December 2018, March 2020 and November 2022.
  • 8.5 million BTC in profit: below the 8.6 million recorded during the FTX collapse in November 2022. It was the first time a drawdown pushed supply in profit below the previous cycle’s low.

Profit and loss are based on UTXO creation prices

Supply in profit is the counterpart to supply in loss: it counts coins whose last on-chain movement happened below the current price. The two measures describe unrealized gains and losses in the supply, rather than realized trading outcomes. A coin can enter the loss category when its market price falls below its last-move price, even if its owner does not transact.

The profit figure adds a different signal to the record loss total. In earlier cycles, CoinDesk reported, more Bitcoin remained in profit at each successive market low. This time, fewer coins were in profit than at the November 2022 FTX collapse low, despite Bitcoin trading at a much higher price than it did then. The metric does not identify which holders are under pressure or predict whether they will sell.

Buying was concentrated in two holder bands

Glassnode’s Accumulation Trend Score showed that entities holding 100 to 1,000 BTC and those holding 1,000 to 10,000 BTC were the only cohorts accumulating over the 15-day period covered by the report. Both groups recorded a score of roughly 0.7. The score approaches 1 when accumulation is stronger and 0 when distribution is stronger.

All other cohorts were distributing, according to the same data. CoinDesk said smaller holders had been selling since September, when Bitcoin traded above $80,000. That split means the aggregate loss reading coincided with buying by two larger balance cohorts and distribution elsewhere; it does not show that the larger entities bought every coin sold by smaller holders.

The 11.6 million figure is therefore a measure of price exposure across on-chain supply, not a count of capitulating investors. It marks how much Bitcoin had a higher last-move price than the market during the dip. Whether those unrealized losses become realized depends on later transactions, which the supply metric alone cannot determine.
