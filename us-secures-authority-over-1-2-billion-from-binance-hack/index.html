US secures authority over $1.2 billion from Binance hack

The Justice Department secured authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6 billion hack, while wider freezes exceed $2.7 billion.

The Finality DeskNov 15, 2026 · 17:32 UTC3 min read#13389e

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step that puts part of the exchange’s reported losses within reach of forfeiture. The Justice Department’s November 12 announcement says government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. Those figures describe different stages: assets frozen by the broader effort, and the subset covered by court authorization.

The department says North Korean military members, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The Associated Press reports the FBI previously attributed the theft to a North Korean hacking group and puts the loss at about $6.3 billion. The Justice Department’s wording is that authorities may “seize or restrain” the $1.2 billion; it does not say that this amount has been returned to Binance or to customers.

That distinction matters. A freeze can prevent assets from being moved while investigators and service providers pursue them. A court-authorized seizure or restraint gives the government legal authority to take or hold identified property. The Associated Press report says the US government typically follows such recoveries with a forfeiture claim before a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to the funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say that a forfeiture judgment has been entered.

The recovery effort involves government agencies and virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. The department says those partners have worked to interdict the stolen assets and that the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery work. It also says investigators are tracing funds as TraderTraitor actors continue to launder them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. These are routes investigators say the actors are using; the announcement does not identify the specific protocols, chains or transactions involved in the $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint.

That leaves the wider freeze total as an incomplete measure of recovery. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through combined public and private action, but the department does not say how much of that sum remains immobilized, how much is covered by the court order, or what share of the stolen assets is still controlled by the attackers. The figures should not be read as proof that all frozen funds are already in government custody or can be distributed to claimants.

The operational target remains fluid because crypto transfers can cross chains and services while investigators pursue legal process and providers act on alerts. Last week, the FBI and its partners issued a cyber alert asking VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses TraderTraitor actors use to launder the Binance assets. The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizing and forfeiting the funds remains ongoing. The announcement gives no final recovery total or timetable for a claims process.

The seizure action was announced alongside a separate disruption of FaceStealer, a North Korean social-engineering scheme that officials say stole more than $400 million over 18 months. The Justice Department explicitly treats that campaign and the Binance theft as separate operations. The Binance figure is tied to the TraderTraitor theft; the FaceStealer figure is not part of the exchange loss or the $1.2 billion seizure authorization.
