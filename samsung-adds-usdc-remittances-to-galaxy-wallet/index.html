Samsung adds USDC remittances to Galaxy Wallet

Samsung will add USDC transfers to Wallet for eligible U.S. Galaxy users in October, reaching crypto wallets or bank accounts in more than 60 countries.

The Finality DeskOct 8, 2026 · 17:09 UTC3 min read#8d8782

Samsung Electronics America will add USDC transfers to Samsung Wallet in the last week of October, letting eligible U.S. Galaxy users send stablecoins to compatible crypto wallets or fund bank payouts in more than 60 countries. The Samsung announcement says the service will be available across 82 million Wallet-compatible U.S. Galaxy devices. It brings stablecoin transfers into an existing mobile wallet, while leaving custody and money movement to partners.

How will a Wallet transfer reach its recipient?

Users will be able to send USDC from Samsung Wallet to compatible crypto wallets or exchange accounts, with Samsung saying those transfers can arrive in seconds. For bank payouts, recipients in more than 60 countries can receive local currency directly into qualifying accounts, without holding a crypto wallet. The exact supported countries and banks will appear in the app; Samsung says eligibility varies.

The two paths have different fees. Samsung says it will not charge for USDC transfers to external wallets, but recipient wallets or exchanges may impose fees. Bank transfers carry fees that vary by destination and amount; the app will show the charge and estimated delivery time before confirmation. Transfer speed to crypto wallets also depends on network conditions.

Samsung names Solana and Sui as infrastructure partners but has not specified which network will handle each transfer or how Wallet selects between them. The Sui Foundation’s announcement says USDC on Sui can transfer without a network fee or requiring the sender to hold SUI. That describes Sui’s gasless transfer support; Samsung’s announcement does not say all Wallet transfers, including bank payouts, will be free.

Who holds the USDC and authorizes transfers?

Bastion will provide the stablecoin accounts, custody and money-movement framework. Samsung says Coinbase will serve as Bastion’s sub-custodian, securing Wallet USDC through Coinbase Prime Vault. Samsung itself says it will not hold customer funds or act as the digital-asset custodian or money transmitter.

To initiate a transfer, users must authenticate biometrically on a registered compatible Galaxy device. The service is limited to eligible U.S. residents aged 18 or older with a Samsung Account. They must complete identity verification and set up biometric authentication. Samsung says users will not need to install another crypto app or manage private keys themselves.

What could prevent a transfer?

A recipient address must belong to a wallet or exchange that supports USDC on a network supported by Samsung Wallet. Samsung says transfers to sanctioned jurisdictions are prohibited. For bank payouts, the receiving account must be eligible, and some banks or account types may not be supported. Delivery times may vary.

Those constraints make this a partner-operated payments service inside Samsung’s interface, rather than a transfer path controlled solely by the phone maker. Users get an app-level entry point and biometric authorization, but availability, custody and settlement depend on the supported networks and financial partners. The October launch begins in the United States; Samsung says expansion into other markets may follow regulatory developments.
