Visa adds Monad for institutional stablecoin settlement

Visa added Monad for participating issuers and acquirers to settle obligations in stablecoins, widening its blockchain options while leaving merchant card flows unchanged.

The Finality DeskNov 11, 2026 · 14:13 UTC3 min read#652c2d

Visa has added the Monad network as an option for participating issuers and acquirers to settle Visa obligations in stablecoins, giving institutions another blockchain rail for moving funds. The Monad Foundation announced the change on November 10 in a release distributed through GlobeNewswire, which quotes Visa vice president Dan Roesbery. The Foundation says the settlement option is available now; the planned privacy feature is still in development.

What changes when an issuer settles on Monad?

Participating issuers and acquirers can use stablecoins on Monad to settle what they owe through Visa. That is a back-end movement of funds between payment institutions. It does not mean cardholders pay merchants in MON or that merchants must accept stablecoins: the Foundation says the Visa experience for merchants and cardholders remains familiar, with settlement taking place behind the scenes.

That distinction matters because card authorization and institutional settlement are separate steps. The announcement describes the latter: institutions gain another network for settling obligations. It does not name which stablecoins are supported, identify any issuer or acquirer using Monad, or provide Monad-specific settlement volumes. Unchained’s report likewise noted that Visa had not published its own announcement at the time and that those implementation details were absent.

Why add a blockchain settlement route?

The Foundation says conventional card settlement has traditionally followed a five-business-day banking schedule. A blockchain settlement route can move funds over weekends and holidays, avoiding that schedule’s calendar constraints. It also says stablecoin settlement on Monad can reduce the operational friction of converting between stablecoins and fiat.

Monad is EVM-compatible, according to the release. The Foundation says issuers and acquirers already using Ethereum-based custody, wallet and compliance tools can typically extend those integrations to Monad rather than build new ones. That describes a potential integration path, not confirmation that any named institution has completed one.

The release attributes sub-second finality to Monad and says transactions can settle moments after submission. It gives network figures of 10,000 transactions per second, 400-millisecond block times and 800-millisecond finality. These are Monad-wide performance claims, not measurements of Visa settlement activity or a guarantee of end-to-end payment timing.

What remains unconfirmed about privacy?

Monad’s Private Settlement feature is planned for the coming months; it is not yet live for Visa partners. The Foundation says it is designed to keep commercially sensitive settlement details confidential while allowing institutions to share them with auditors and regulators. It also says the feature will run directly on Monad, keeping private activity connected to the network’s shared liquidity. The announcement does not specify the cryptographic design or access-control mechanism.

Visa reported an annualized stablecoin settlement run rate of $25 billion as of September 2026, roughly 10 times the year-earlier level, alongside more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live globally, according to the Foundation’s release. Those figures describe Visa’s overall stablecoin activity, not usage of Monad. The addition expands the available network choices; actual adoption, supported stablecoins and settlement volume on Monad remain undisclosed.
