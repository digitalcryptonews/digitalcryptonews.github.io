Coinbase Clearing completes its CFTC-regulated derivatives stack

Coinbase Clearing LLC is now a CFTC-registered clearing organization, letting Coinbase clear fully collateralized futures, options and swaps within its US derivatives stack.

The Finality DeskOct 4, 2026 · 13:41 UTC3 min read#e02fbe

Coinbase completed its CFTC-regulated US derivatives stack on September 28, when the Commodity Futures Trading Commission registered Coinbase Clearing LLC as a derivatives clearing organization (DCO). The CFTC registration order permits the entity to clear fully collateralized futures, options on futures and swaps. Coinbase already operates a futures commission merchant (FCM), Coinbase Financial Markets, and a designated contract market (DCM), Coinbase Derivatives.

The new registration adds clearing to those brokerage and exchange functions. Coinbase says the DCO lets it create and settle fully collateralized contracts directly. That gives the company an in-house path from listing through brokerage to clearing for products within the DCO’s permitted scope. It does not mean every Coinbase derivatives product will use Coinbase Clearing.

Which products can Coinbase Clearing clear?

The CFTC’s order limits Coinbase Clearing’s permission to fully collateralized futures, options on futures and swaps. It does not authorize the DCO to clear margined products. Coinbase describes the clearinghouse as USDC-native, with USDC collateral and 24/7 settlement. The registration makes that infrastructure available for the product classes listed in the CFTC record; Coinbase has not said in its announcement which contracts it will launch first.

Collateralization is the key boundary. The products Coinbase Clearing can handle must be fully collateralized. According to The Block’s report on the approval, margined derivatives will continue to go through outside clearing partners. Coinbase also said it will use existing partners for its margined derivatives business and planned single-stock perpetuals. The DCO registration therefore adds a direct clearing route for fully funded contracts while leaving those other products on partner paths.

What responsibilities come with DCO registration?

The CFTC order records Coinbase’s commitments as a registered clearing organization. Coinbase represented that it will monitor and enforce clearing members’ compliance with admission and continuing eligibility standards, as well as compliance with its rules and procedures. It also represented that funds held in clearing-member accounts will be treated as member property and kept separate from Coinbase’s own funds.

The order says CFTC staff reviewed Coinbase’s application, amendments, rules and representations, and technically evaluated its proposed operations against the applicable core principles and regulations. The Commission concluded that the application demonstrated compliance with the requirements for DCO registration. The order is based on the representations and supporting material Coinbase provided; it says changes or omissions in material facts or circumstances may require a new or amended order.

Does the approval complete Coinbase’s derivatives stack?

It completes the company’s set of CFTC-regulated roles, not its handling of every product through one entity. The FCM intermediates customer access, the DCM lists and operates the derivatives market, and the DCO clears eligible contracts. Coinbase now has an entity for each role: Coinbase Financial Markets, Coinbase Derivatives and Coinbase Clearing, respectively.

That structure lets Coinbase bring some fully collateralized products through its own clearinghouse rather than relying on a third-party clearer. But the authorization’s product limits matter: margined derivatives and the announced single-stock perpetual launch remain outside Coinbase Clearing’s stated scope. Coinbase says direct creation and settlement can make product development and operations more efficient; the approval itself does not announce a new contract, launch date or change to existing products.
