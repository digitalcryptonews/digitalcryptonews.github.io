Fairshake Names 32 House Incumbents for Midterm Spending

Fairshake named 32 House incumbents for support, including six slated for $1 million each, as the CLARITY Act remains stalled ahead of November's midterms.

The Finality DeskOct 8, 2026 · 08:31 UTC3 min read#c1b95d

Fairshake announced support for 32 House incumbents in the November midterms, putting $1 million behind six members who backed the crypto industry’s market-structure bill as it stalled in the Senate. The slate has 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. The Associated Press report says Fairshake did not disclose planned spending on the other 26 candidates.

Which House members are receiving $1 million?

Three Democrats and three Republicans are slated for that level of support. The Democrats are Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. The Republicans are French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. All six are incumbents, according to Roll Call’s account of Fairshake’s announcement.

The other 26 names are also incumbents: 10 Democrats and 16 Republicans. They include House Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar, Majority Whip Tom Emmer and Ways and Means Chairman Jason Smith. Fairshake has not provided individual spending amounts for those races, so the $6 million committed to the six named recipients is the only disclosed total for this House slate.

That distinction matters for reading the size of the effort. Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, the AP reported, but the group has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate races before November. Its separate plan to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio is substantially larger than the six disclosed House commitments.

How does Fairshake’s support reach the campaigns?

Fairshake is a super PAC, and the announced support takes the form of outside spending rather than coordinated campaign spending. CoinDesk reported that Fairshake’s spending has gone to independent advertising buys outside candidates’ involvement. That mechanism lets the group pay for its own political communications without routing the money through a candidate’s campaign. The announcement does not specify which ads or other expenditures it plans for each of the 32 House races.

The group says its selection rule is support for crypto policy, not party. Spokesperson Geoff Vetter told the AP that Fairshake backs pro-crypto candidates in both parties. The slate’s two-party composition is consistent with that stated approach, though it does not establish how much each candidate will receive beyond the six disclosed commitments.

What legislation has the group tied to its spending?

All 32 candidates supported the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, according to the AP. The House passed the measure in 2025; it would establish a federal framework for digital-asset markets and divide oversight between regulators. The Senate blocked it on a procedural vote in September after lawmakers failed to agree on provisions including ethics rules for public officials with crypto interests.

That stalled bill sets the policy context for Fairshake’s election spending. The group is backing House members who supported the proposal while the industry looks to a future Congress to revive it. Vetter also told the AP that Fairshake and its affiliated groups spent $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year. Those figures describe earlier races, not the yet-undisclosed spending on 26 candidates in the new House slate.
