WBTC vs tBTC vs cbBTC: Your First DeFi Deposit

WBTC vs tBTC vs cbBTC for beginners: choose the token your app supports for simpler BTC redemption, or tBTC if you prefer distributed custody and its extra steps.

The Finality DeskNov 16, 2026 · 22:18 UTC5 min read#e72471

Wrapped bitcoin tokens let you use BTC value in DeFi on another blockchain without selling your bitcoin. For most beginners, cbBTC or WBTC is the practical pick when the exchange or app they already use supports it: fewer transfer steps mean fewer chances to choose the wrong network or send funds to the wrong address. Coinbase describes cbBTC as backed 1:1 by BTC it holds in custody, while the Coinbase cbBTC announcement explains how it represents Bitcoin on supported chains. Choose tBTC if avoiding a single company’s control matters more to you than a simpler setup.

What happens when you wrap 1 BTC?

Wrapping 1 BTC means depositing bitcoin into a custody arrangement and receiving a token on another blockchain that represents it. The token can then interact with apps on that chain, such as lending or trading protocols. To return to bitcoin, you redeem or unwrap the token through the system that issued it.

With cbBTC, Coinbase holds the bitcoin and issues the wrapped token. When cbBTC is deposited back into an eligible Coinbase account, Coinbase says it converts the wrapped balance to BTC. With WBTC, BTC is held through its custodian and merchant process; the WBTC participant documentation lists BitGo as custodian. A merchant requests minting or redemption, while the custodian handles the underlying bitcoin.

tBTC uses a different custody mechanism. You send BTC to a deposit address controlled by a Threshold Network wallet. A selected signer group must cooperate to move the bitcoin; Threshold’s tBTC documentation describes a randomly selected group of operators and a threshold agreement for wallet actions. Once the deposit is recognized, the system issues tBTC on a supported chain. A single signer cannot move the bitcoin alone.

Which one is easier to use?

For a first DeFi deposit, the token already supported by your exchange, wallet, and target app is usually the easiest choice. That can be cbBTC or WBTC. You can withdraw or convert BTC into the supported wrapped token, connect the wallet to the app, and later redeem through the issuer’s supported route. Check the network at each step: a token on Base, for example, is not interchangeable with a token sent on Ethereum just because both represent bitcoin.

tBTC reduces reliance on one company by distributing control of bitcoin movement across Threshold signers. That design comes with extra steps: create a deposit, send BTC to the specified Bitcoin address, wait for the deposit process, then receive tBTC on the destination chain. Redemption also depends on the signer group forming the required threshold. A delay or unavailable signer quorum can slow a wallet action. So tBTC can fit a user who values distributed control and is comfortable following a bridge flow, but it is not automatically the simplest first token.

Who controls the BTC in each case?

The key difference is who can authorize movement of the bitcoin and process redemption. Take a person wrapping 1 BTC. With cbBTC, Coinbase is the custody and redemption counterparty: the holder relies on Coinbase to hold the BTC and process conversion back to BTC. With WBTC, BitGo is listed as custodian, and redemption runs through WBTC’s merchant and custody process. In either case, a holder does not personally sign a Bitcoin transaction from the custodian’s reserve.

With tBTC, the underlying bitcoin sits in a wallet controlled by a threshold signer group. The group must meet the protocol’s signing threshold to move funds. That removes a single company as the sole controller, but adds dependence on the protocol, its contracts, and enough signers being available. In practical terms, centralized custody can make redemption more familiar; distributed signing changes who must cooperate and can add operational complexity.

RenBridge is a useful comparison because it also moved bitcoin value across chains by issuing a wrapped representation. The renbridge.net describes RenBridge’s cross-chain service, illustrating the same basic dependency: a wrapped token relies on the system behind it staying operational. A wrapped token is only useful if its custody and redemption path still works. That is why the choice involves more than checking whether a token says “BTC” in a wallet.

How should a beginner choose?

Pick cbBTC or WBTC when your current exchange or DeFi app supports it directly and you want fewer transfer steps. Pick tBTC when distributed signer control is a priority and you are ready to follow its deposit and redemption process. Before sending, confirm the exact token and network in the receiving app, then check that you understand where redemption happens.

RenBridge’s history is a reminder to include the issuer or protocol in that check: a wrapped token depends on its operating system remaining active. The working tip is simple: use the route your app supports directly, and verify the network before each transfer. That one pause can spare you from sending a valid token to an address or chain that cannot use it.
