US courts authorize action on $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

Courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion linked to Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, while authorities and private firms continue tracing funds.

The Finality DeskNov 14, 2026 · 12:40 UTC3 min read#bd307a

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The department said government and private-sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, while tracing and recovery work continues.

The Justice Department announcement says the theft occurred on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The FBI attributes it to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The department’s wording distinguishes the court-authorized amount from the larger pool already frozen: the $1.2 billion is part of the $2.7 billion total, not an additional recovery.

Those figures describe different stages of the response. The announcement says law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the funds. It reports that providers and government partners froze assets, and that the department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain a portion of them. It does not say the full $1.2 billion has been transferred to government custody or returned to Binance or affected customers.

The legal status matters because a freeze can restrict movement without resolving ownership. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in such cases, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department has not announced the outcome of such a process for these assets.

Authorities say the recovery operation is still active because TraderTraitor actors are laundering the stolen cryptocurrency through bridges, mixers and exchanges. The Justice Department named those routes but did not publish transaction-level details in its announcement. It said the FBI, the Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery efforts, while the FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions with or derived from addresses the actors are using.

That cooperation is central to the scale of the freeze reported so far. The department credited its National Security Cyber Section, the Criminal Division’s Computer Crime and Intellectual Property Section, the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia and law enforcement partners with working alongside VASPs. The announcement does not identify which providers froze particular assets or specify how much of the total sits on each network or in each asset.

The $2.7 billion figure is therefore a cumulative freeze attributed to government and private-sector action, while the court-authorized $1.2 billion figure is a legal step concerning part of that pool. Neither figure establishes how much will ultimately be forfeited or returned. Authorities say efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets remain ongoing as the laundering path continues across services.
