Binance rolls out AI interface after 150,000-user beta

Binance opened its AI interface to English-language users after a beta with about 150,000 testers, adding personalized market context, alerts and portfolio summaries.

The Finality DeskOct 13, 2026 · 08:45 UTC3 min read#d8f0b9

Binance has rolled out Binance AI, a personalized trading interface, to global English-language users after a beta that drew about 150,000 testers. The Block’s report says the beta began on Sept. 7; Binance’s announcement describes an interface that adapts market information and tools to users’ interests and experience. The product’s role is to organize and explain information inside Binance, not to make investment decisions for users.

What does Binance AI change in the trading interface?

Binance AI changes which information and tools appear on screen. Binance says its AI-generated layouts adapt modules and widgets to a user’s needs and the assets they are exploring. A newer user may see simpler explanations and prompts; active traders can access deeper market context, data views and product-specific modules. Users can customize the widgets and reconfigure the layout.

The interface also ties information to assets, products and market themes a user follows. Its “Top movers” feature shows 24-hour price movements across a user’s holdings with AI-generated analysis personalized to the assets they hold and follow. “Yesterday’s PNL” recaps the previous day’s performance alongside events affecting top holdings. Smart alerts can use voice to notify users when tracked assets move. On-demand market briefs provide updates in text or audio, refreshed every four hours, according to Binance.

These features put summaries and selected market data in the trading interface. They do not, by themselves, establish that Binance AI places orders or executes a strategy. The company’s announcement frames the product around layouts, context, prompts, alerts and market briefs. Its wording matters: this rollout is an adaptive interface for market information, rather than a disclosed automated trading agent.

Who can use the rollout, and where?

Binance says the features are progressively rolling out to eligible users on its app and website. The Block reported that the rollout covers global English-language users, while support for additional languages is still being added. The official Binance announcement says app users should update to the latest version; personalized features then begin adapting to their usage. The company cautions that products may not be available in every region.

The beta announcement, published Sept. 7, invited verified users to register interest. Binance said spaces were limited, access would be granted on a rolling basis, and registration did not guarantee selection. The Block reported that about 150,000 users tested the product during the beta. That is the reported tester count; neither source gives a breakdown of how many testers became active users or how they used individual features.

What are the limits of the AI-generated output?

Binance’s terms put responsibility for prompts and investment decisions on the user. The company warns that inputs may include unvetted third-party material and that outputs can contain errors, bias, synthetic data or outdated information. It says outputs are not advice and should not be relied on alone for decisions. It also does not guarantee that a response will satisfy a request.

Those limits apply directly to a tool that summarizes market activity and personal holdings: a concise explanation can omit context, and an alert about a price move is not an assessment of whether to trade. Binance says it may apply compliance and safety filters to sourced content, but that filtering is not absolute. The rollout adds an AI layer to how users view and navigate market information. Users remain responsible for checking the underlying data and deciding whether to act.
