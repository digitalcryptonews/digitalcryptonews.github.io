DOJ obtains seizure authority for $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, within a wider freeze effort that has immobilized over $2.7 billion.

The Finality DeskNov 17, 2026 · 17:16 UTC3 min read#402ff7

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, as tracing and recovery efforts continue across several routes used to launder the assets. The Justice Department’s announcement says the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts.

The FBI attributes the theft, which took place on or about Oct. 29, to members of the North Korean military it calls TraderTraitor. The department says the attackers stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The Associated Press reported that the FBI had previously attributed the attack to TraderTraitor and that the U.S. government had obtained court authorization covering more than $1.2 billion of the frozen funds.

The figures describe different stages of intervention. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen so far through collective U.S. government and private-sector work. The Justice Department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion within that frozen amount. The authorization does not mean the government has completed forfeiture or returned the assets to Binance users. The AP report on the seizure says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The distinction matters because freezing, seizing and forfeiting are separate steps. A freeze can prevent assets held by a cooperating service from moving. A court order can authorize authorities to seize or restrain specified assets. Forfeiture determines whether the government can take ownership under law, while any return to victims is a further question. The Justice Department’s release does not identify the particular tokens, wallet addresses or court filings covered by the $1.2 billion authorization.

Authorities say the remaining recovery work involves tracing assets as TraderTraitor moves them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services create different points of intervention: bridges move value between blockchain networks, mixers obscure transaction trails, and exchanges may hold assets in accounts that can be frozen. The department says law enforcement has worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert urging those providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses used to launder Binance funds.

The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets remain ongoing. That leaves a gap between the headline figure and money actually returned: authorities have described a court-authorized amount and a broader frozen pool, but have not announced a final forfeiture or distribution to users. The FBI’s attribution and the government’s recovery figures are official claims; the public release does not provide transaction-level evidence or a breakdown of how much of the stolen value remains accessible to law enforcement.
