1inch Adds Hyperliquid Perpetuals to Its dApp and Android Wallet

1inch added Hyperliquid perpetuals to its web dApp and Android wallet, routing supported tokens through USDC into Hyperliquid while keeping execution there.

The Finality DeskNov 20, 2026 · 23:51 UTC3 min read#348632

1inch has added Hyperliquid perpetual futures to its web dApp and Android wallet, letting eligible users open and manage positions through its interface while Hyperliquid runs the markets. In its launch announcement, 1inch said the integration also adds a funding flow: a user can swap a supported token into USDC and deposit it to a perps balance on Hyperliquid.

The launch, announced November 19, is available in eligible regions. The Block reported the rollout covers the dApp and Android wallet; 1inch says iOS support is planned for a later release. The interface consolidates access and position monitoring, but it does not move execution onto 1inch: Hyperliquid operates the order books and handles pricing, funding payments and liquidations.

USDC funds the Hyperliquid perps balance

Users can fund a position with a supported token they already hold in 1inch. The integration swaps that asset into USDC, then deposits the USDC to a balance on Hyperliquid. That balance backs the user’s positions there. Free collateral can be withdrawn as USDC; the announcement lists withdrawal routes for 1inch.com, while wallet withdrawal-chain support is still pending.

The distinction matters when a position is open. According to 1inch, collateral deposited for perps is held by Hyperliquid, not in the user’s wallet or by 1inch. 1inch supplies the interface, funding flow and on-device signing. It says it does not operate the markets, act as a counterparty, manage positions or guarantee their outcomes.

Margin defaults change the collateral at risk

The wallet and web dApp use different default margin modes. The Android wallet defaults to isolated margin, where a position is backed by the margin allocated to it. The dApp defaults to cross margin: the perps balance backs all open positions, so losses on one can draw on the balance supporting the others. Users can set leverage per market.

Both interfaces support market, limit and time-weighted average price (TWAP) orders. A TWAP order splits a larger order into smaller trades over a chosen period. Traders can add take-profit or stop-loss instructions at entry or later; 1inch says those instructions close the position with a market order when the specified price is reached. Users can also add to, reduce, reverse or close positions.

Markets and access remain subject to limits

The integration exposes Hyperliquid markets for crypto, stocks, commodities and foreign exchange, including HIP-3 markets, which track stocks, commodities and currencies. Users do not need to own the referenced asset to take a position. The 1inch interface also displays charts, order books, recent trades and funding rates, alongside positions and trading activity.

Perpetuals have no expiry date, but positions remain subject to margin requirements, funding payments and liquidation. The default margin setting affects how much collateral may be exposed when losses accumulate. 1inch says access is unavailable in the United States, the United Kingdom, Ontario and Belgium, as well as other restricted jurisdictions. The launch adds an entry point and management tools inside 1inch; Hyperliquid remains responsible for the trading venue and collateral deposited there.
