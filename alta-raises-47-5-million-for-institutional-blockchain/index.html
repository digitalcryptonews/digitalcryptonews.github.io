Alta raises $47.5 million for institutional blockchain

Alta raised a $47.5 million Series A to build a blockchain using recursive zero-knowledge cryptography, with Airwallex set to connect payment rails and stablecoin flows.

The Finality DeskNov 12, 2026 · 04:05 UTC3 min read#aebaa5

Alta raised $47.5 million in a Series A led by Index Ventures to build blockchain infrastructure for financial institutions, the company said in its funding announcement. The round brings its total funding to $80 million, including $32.5 million in earlier seed financing. GV, Blackbird Ventures, Square Peg and Capital 49 also participated. Alta says its chain will combine transaction privacy with programmable compliance, targeting banks and payment firms that need to verify transfers without exposing sensitive financial data.

How does Alta combine privacy with compliance?

Alta says its blockchain architecture uses recursive zero-knowledge cryptography to keep balances, transactions, identities and compliance policies private while verifying that transactions satisfy required rules. The announcement does not describe the proof system’s circuit design, who generates or verifies proofs, or how policy updates are authorized. It therefore establishes the intended privacy and compliance properties, but not the low-level mechanism or operational limits that will govern them.

The design puts policy enforcement into the blockchain itself, according to CEO and co-founder Loong Wang. Alta describes the system as supporting transactions across assets, chains and jurisdictions, with identity and authorization built into its infrastructure. Those are company claims about the planned architecture; the announcement does not specify a consensus protocol, transaction ordering model, or how the chain handles a transfer that fails a policy check.

Fortune reported a more specific control: Wang said Alta includes a feature to prevent arbitrary parties from sending funds to an address without the recipient’s consent, a behavior he contrasted with unsolicited token airdrops on many blockchains. That account does not identify the function or protocol rule that implements the restriction. The feature matters for institutional assets because transaction receipt itself can create compliance and operational issues, but its precise enforcement path remains undisclosed.

What will Airwallex connect to the chain?

Airwallex, the payments company co-founded by Alta co-founder Jack Zhang, has chosen to build on Alta and is an early design partner. The planned connection links Airwallex’s global payment infrastructure to Alta’s blockchain. Alta says the collaboration will support stablecoin pay-ins and payouts, as well as access to local fiat payment networks across 200 countries and 90 currencies. The announcement does not name the stablecoins, bridges, or settlement contracts involved.

The initial product is aimed at businesses managing liquidity across payment networks. Alta says it will use tokenized assets to support just-in-time funding, foreign exchange at settlement, and movement into and out of yield-bearing tokenized assets. The intended effect is to reduce cash held in prefunded accounts: money can be deployed when needed instead of sitting idle as a buffer. Alta cites an estimated $4 trillion tied up in prefunded accounts globally, but the announcement does not state how much of that market its product could address.

What does the funding support?

Alta says the capital will fund continued blockchain development, institutional partnerships, global financial connectivity, and hiring in engineering and compliance. The company did not disclose a valuation. Its near-term test is whether it can connect the promised privacy and policy controls to working payment flows through partners such as Airwallex. That will depend on details not yet public, including the chain’s transaction validation rules and how institutions configure and enforce their policies.
