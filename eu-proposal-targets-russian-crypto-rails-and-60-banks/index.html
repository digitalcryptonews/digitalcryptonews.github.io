EU proposal targets Russian crypto rails and 60 banks

The European Commission has proposed adding crypto and payment networks to Russia sanctions, alongside 60 banks and restrictions on Russian participation in EU sport.

The Finality DeskNov 26, 2026 · 13:51 UTC3 min read#2643d5

The European Commission has proposed a 22nd sanctions package against Russia that would target crypto and payment networks, extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and restrict Russian participation in EU sports. The package is a proposal, not an adopted measure: all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can take effect, the Associated Press reported.

What does the proposal say about crypto?

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would crack down on Russian crypto and payment networks, and could include third countries that help Russia evade existing sanctions. She also announced a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work, according to the European Commission statement on the package.

The statement does not name a token, exchange, wallet provider, blockchain, payment route or specific transaction rule. It therefore sets out the intended scope and enforcement focus, but not the crypto provisions’ operational terms. It does not say whether the proposal would bar transactions with named services, impose a wider sectoral ban, or create new obligations for intermediaries. Those details cannot be inferred from the announcement.

How would the bank measure work?

The Commission proposes extending a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the financial messaging system used for international payments. A transaction ban restricts dealings with the designated institutions; removal from SWIFT cuts their access to that messaging network. The announcement does not identify the banks or spell out exceptions, implementation dates or transition arrangements.

The measures are meant to constrain financial channels used to fund Russia’s war and to make sanctions evasion harder. The crypto component names networks and third-country assistance as targets, but the statement does not detail how investigators would identify or attribute those connections. The proposed task force signals an enforcement effort; the Commission’s announcement does not specify its membership, powers or procedures.

What else is included in the 22nd package?

The proposal also covers energy services, shipping, visas, company governance and sport. The Commission says it would bar European companies from helping build, operate or repair Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries, and extend the services ban to electricity. It proposes listing more than 70 shadow-fleet ships and banning tankers insured in Russia from the relevant activity.

On sport and culture, the proposal would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at events in the EU, prohibit the display of official Russian symbols, restrict Russian sponsorship flows to European-based sports and cultural associations, and prevent Russians from holding posts in governing bodies of European sports federations. It would also bar Europeans from continuing to hold governing-body posts at companies in Russia and introduce a visa ban for Russian ex-combatants.

When could the measures take effect?

Not until EU governments agree. The Commission presented the package on 23 November; AP reported that the approval process can take weeks and that measures may change during negotiations. Until member states endorse the package and the measures enter into force, the announced crypto and banking restrictions remain proposed policy. The announcement provides no timetable for a final vote or for implementation.
