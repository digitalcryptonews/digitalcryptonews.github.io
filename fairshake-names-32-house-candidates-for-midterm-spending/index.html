Fairshake names 32 House candidates for midterm spending

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for midterm support, with $1 million in spending for six, after Senate negotiations over the CLARITY Act broke down.

The Finality DeskOct 7, 2026 · 04:58 UTC3 min read#deea90

Fairshake, the crypto industry’s major political spending group, named 32 House candidates it will support in November’s midterms, putting money behind lawmakers who backed digital-asset legislation that stalled in the Senate. The Associated Press report says the slate has 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. Six candidates are slated for $1 million each.

Which House candidates are getting the disclosed $1 million?

Fairshake plans $1 million in support for three Democrats: Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. The three Republicans are French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan, according to Roll Call’s account of the PAC’s announcement.

Hill chairs the House Financial Services Committee, Steil chairs its digital assets subcommittee, and Huizenga is the panel’s vice chair. The other 26 candidates include House leaders and committee members, among them Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar and Republican Majority Whip Tom Emmer. Fairshake has not disclosed how much it plans to spend supporting those 26 candidates, so the six $1 million allocations do not establish a total for the House slate.

What does Fairshake mean by “support”?

The reported amounts are spending to support candidates, not funds paid directly to their campaign committees. Fairshake is a political action committee, and the coverage describes its House effort as outside spending. The distinction matters: the $1 million figures describe the group’s planned election activity on each candidate’s behalf, not campaign receipts the candidates can spend themselves.

Fairshake’s slate covers both parties, but its stated selection tracks support for crypto policy. All 32 candidates backed the market-structure legislation the industry prioritized. Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter told AP that the group backs candidates in both parties who support what it calls American innovation. The PAC’s broader spending network reported about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, AP reported; the figure is not a disclosed budget for this House slate.

Why did the CLARITY Act stall in the Senate?

The House passed the CLARITY Act in July 2025. The bill sought a federal regulatory framework for digital-asset markets and a division of oversight among federal regulators. It did not advance after Senate Democrats helped block it in September, following disagreement over ethics protections for public officials with crypto interests, among other issues.

That procedural defeat leaves the bill’s supporters without the Senate agreement needed to move the framework forward this year. Fairshake’s House slate includes lawmakers who supported the measure, giving the group a way to back aligned members as it looks to the next Congress. The spending also comes alongside a separate, larger Senate effort: AP reported that Fairshake planned nearly $30 million in spending against Democratic candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio after the CLARITY Act vote.

Fairshake has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate contests before November. The unresolved figure is material: the disclosed six million dollars shows the minimum specified for six races, while the group’s total commitment across 32 House campaigns remains unknown.
