Circle plans entry into about 40 emerging markets

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire says the issuer will pursue about 40 emerging markets as rising stablecoin demand pushes USDC toward mainstream use globally.

The Finality DeskOct 16, 2026 · 15:20 UTC4 min read#ba0a36

Circle plans to enter about 40 emerging and developing countries, CEO Jeremy Allaire told Reuters on Oct. 16, citing rising demand for stablecoins across several regions. The expansion would take the USDC issuer into markets where individuals and businesses are seeking digital dollars and other internet based financial services, he said. Reuters’ report on Allaire’s remarks places the push in Southeast Asia, Latin America, Africa and the Middle East.

Allaire spoke in Bangkok on the sidelines of the IMF and World Bank meetings. He described the shift as blockchain moving from early adopters to mainstream scale. The roughly 40 country target is a plan, not a list of launches: Reuters did not identify the countries, local partners, or a rollout timetable. Circle’s page says USDC is already available in more than 185 countries, so the new target describes an expansion push rather than the stablecoin’s first availability in those markets.

What would Circle need to add in each market?

Local access depends on more than putting a token on a blockchain. USDC moves across supported networks, but users generally get it through exchanges, neobanks, wallets or other on and off ramp providers. Circle says its direct Circle Mint service is for institutions and allows eligible businesses to exchange dollars for USDC and redeem USDC for dollars.

That issuance and redemption process anchors the token’s dollar value. When a business deposits dollars through Circle Mint, Circle issues the equivalent amount of USDC; when it redeems USDC for dollars, Circle removes those tokens from circulation. Circle says USDC is backed by cash and cash equivalent assets and redeemable one to one for U.S. dollars. Circle’s USDC page describes the mechanics and access routes.

For expansion into a new country, distribution and local conversion matter alongside on-chain settlement. A user may be able to hold or transfer USDC, but converting between USDC and local currency depends on available payment providers and banking connections. Reuters did not report which partners Circle will use in the roughly 40 target countries or what services will launch there.

How large is USDC’s current base?

Reuters reported that USDC circulation rose 19% year over year to $73.3 billion in the second quarter. It put Circle’s share of the global stablecoin market at roughly 25%, compared with nearly 60% for Tether’s USDT. Those figures set the scale of the expansion: Circle is seeking more distribution for a large existing dollar token in a market where one rival still holds the larger share.

Reuters also cited Circle’s $400 million acquisition of payments firm Tazapay and a $100 million strategic investment from Binance as support for its global expansion. The report did not specify how either would serve the new country push. Allaire linked demand to businesses and individuals seeking digital utility and access to an internet based economy; that is his account of the trend, not a country by country measure of USDC use.

What could limit the rollout?

Circle’s plan leaves open the practical constraints that determine whether demand becomes regular usage: compliant local distribution, reliable conversion to local money, and integrations with businesses or payment firms. The Reuters report says banks and payment companies are also developing stablecoins and digital asset services in the United States and Europe. That competition makes access and settlement routes part of the expansion, not just the token itself.

Regulation is another factor. Reuters reported that Allaire pointed to clearer frameworks, including the U.S. GENIUS Act, as a driver for adoption, and said Circle received a federal bank charter in July. Those developments may shape institutional access, but the report does not establish what rules or licenses Circle will need in each target country. The next concrete signal will be named markets and local distribution arrangements; until then, the 40 country figure remains a strategic target.
