SEC staff calls Canton Coin a digital commodity

SEC staff views Canton Coin as a digital commodity and will not recommend enforcement over specified programmatic minting, tying both positions to facts Canton supplied.

The Finality DeskNov 5, 2026 · 18:57 UTC4 min read#caf0f6

The SEC’s Division of Corporation Finance said it views Canton Coin as a “digital commodity” and will not recommend enforcement over specified programmatic minting, giving the network’s token a staff position under federal securities laws. In its Nov. 3 response on Canton Coin, the division said it relied on representations in a letter submitted for Digital Asset, Canton’s creator. It cited the token’s connection to the network’s programmatic operation and supply-and-demand dynamics, and the network’s reported lack of a “central party.”

The response is conditional. Staff said it would not recommend enforcement if Super Validators, Validators and application providers mint Canton Coin programmatically in the manner described in the request, without registration under Section 5 of the Securities Act or Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act. The division warned that different facts could change its view. It also said the response expresses an enforcement position only and gives no legal conclusion on the issues raised.

What did SEC staff say about the token?

Staff said Canton Coin fits the “digital commodity” category as described in the SEC’s crypto interpretation. Its reasoning rests on facts represented by Digital Asset’s counsel, not an independent finding about the network. The request said the token’s value derives from the network’s operation and supply and demand, rather than an expectation of profits from others’ essential managerial efforts, and that no central party controls the network.

The distinction matters: the response does not say the SEC Commission voted to classify Canton Coin, nor does it make a legal determination that the token is not a security. The staff expressly limited its response to enforcement and to the facts presented. Decrypt’s report on the letter likewise notes that the relief concerns staff’s enforcement position and relies on Canton’s representations.

How does Canton Coin minting work?

Digital Asset’s request describes minting as protocol-driven and tied to measured network activity. Super Validators operate synchronization infrastructure; Validators process transactions to which they are parties; application providers earn rewards when their applications generate activity. The request says participants mint against protocol-generated activity records, rather than receiving rewards distributed by a sponsor. These are the submitted account of the system’s mechanics that underpins the staff response.

The request also says Canton Coin pays fees for the Global Synchronizer, which sequences and confirms transactions across the network. Fees are denominated in U.S. dollars but paid by burning Canton Coin; new coins enter circulation through protocol-defined minting rounds as rewards for activity. That burn-and-mint design is central to the argument that the token is linked to network use. Staff cited the broader claim that value derives from programmatic operation, but did not separately verify each mechanism in its short response.

What limits the relief?

The no-action position applies to the described programmatic minting by the three participant groups and the specified registration provisions. It is not a general authorization for every Canton Coin transaction or a ruling on secondary-market trading. Nor does it remove the conditions in the request: the response says materially different facts may lead staff to a different conclusion.

Governance is part of those submitted facts. Digital Asset’s request says protocol changes, including economic parameters, require approval from at least two-thirds of Super Validator Node Operators; the Foundation’s committees can discuss changes but cannot enact them. The staff response pointed to the absence of a central party as a reason for its view, while leaving the factual basis and the limits of that view attached to the request. For network participants, the practical effect is narrow: staff has signaled it will not recommend enforcement over the described minting arrangement when it operates under the represented conditions.
