US wins court authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, while providers have frozen $2.7 billion.

The Finality DeskNov 18, 2026 · 00:36 UTC3 min read#5ff2c2

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a subset of the $2.7 billion frozen by government and private-sector efforts since the October theft. The department said the recovery work is ongoing; The Associated Press reported the announcement on November 12.

The figures describe different stages of asset recovery. The $2.7 billion is the amount frozen so far, according to the Justice Department. The $1.2 billion is the portion for which the department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain. That authorization does not mean the full amount has already been transferred to government-controlled wallets or forfeited. The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets continue.

The theft took place on or about October 29. The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI tracks as TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. In its October 31 alert, the FBI said it had observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether. It expected further laundering through decentralized finance and instant-swap services.

That movement across services is central to the recovery effort. The Justice Department’s announcement says law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds. The FBI, the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation urged private-sector operators—including DeFi services, instant exchanges, THORNode and RPC node operators—to block transactions with or derived from addresses used by TraderTraitor. The department says the actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Those requests rely on intermediaries’ ability to identify and act on transactions associated with listed addresses. The FBI published addresses it says are holding or have held assets from the theft, and asked service operators to block related flows. The department has not described in its announcement which specific providers froze each tranche, which assets make up the court-authorized amount, or whether the authorized assets have since been moved. The public figures therefore mark a combination of intervention and legal process, not a final accounting of recovered funds.

For forfeiture, the next question is who has a legitimate claim to assets recovered by the government. AP reported that the U.S. typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines legitimate claims to the funds. The Justice Department says its actions to trace and recover the Binance assets are still underway. It has not said how much of the $2.7 billion frozen total will ultimately be forfeited or returned.
