HSBC RedCoin starts phased rollout to PayMe users

HSBC has begun a phased PayMe rollout of its HKD stablecoin, giving 3.3 million users access to app-based transfers and merchant payments under HKMA licensing.

The Finality DeskOct 26, 2026 · 07:20 UTC3 min read#88c4c2

HSBC began rolling out RedCoin, its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, through PayMe on 23 October, bringing in-app stablecoin transfers and merchant payments to a potential base of more than 3.3 million users. The bank says its launch announcement puts the token, ticker RCHKD, under a stablecoin issuer licence from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). Wallet activation is phased: HSBC said users would be able to activate wallets over the following days.

The initial payment paths run through PayMe. Users can send RedCoin to friends and family, or pay participating merchants in the app. HSBC says the merchant network covers more than 100,000 locations. The South China Morning Post’s report, citing PayMe head Lawrence Li, says customers can select the RedCoin wallet after updating the app; it requires no minimum balance or additional account opening.

How does RedCoin reach PayMe users?

HSBC is adding a stablecoin wallet to an existing payments app, rather than requiring users to open a separate crypto wallet. In the launch announcement, the bank describes in-app wallet activation, peer-to-peer transfers and peer-to-merchant payments. It also says a staff pilot of nearly 100 users tested the journey from minting through wallet activation to live transfers.

That describes the customer and payment flow, but not the token’s technical settlement layer. HSBC’s launch announcement names no blockchain, token standard, smart contract or external wallet route. It therefore does not establish whether RedCoin can move beyond HSBC’s apps or interoperate with public crypto networks. The confirmed launch scope is PayMe, with the bank’s Hong Kong mobile banking app named for a later phase.

RedCoin is HKD-denominated and licensed by the HKMA. The licence places issuance within Hong Kong’s regulatory framework for stablecoins. HSBC says the token is designed for everyday payments and that its financial crime controls meet requirements under the Stablecoins Ordinance. Those facts identify the issuer and permitted initial use; they do not disclose the chain, transaction finality rule or redemption process used by the wallet.

What changes after the PayMe launch?

The immediate change is access to a stablecoin balance inside an app already used for payments. PayMe users can use RedCoin for the two launch cases HSBC specifies: transfers to other people and payments to merchants. The rollout does not, on the available disclosures, show RedCoin trading on exchanges or transferring to self-custody wallets.

HSBC plans to add wallet functionality to its Hong Kong mobile banking app later this year. It says customers will then be able to subscribe to tokenised investment products using RedCoin, including a planned share class of the bank’s HKD money market fund. That extension would connect the payment token to investment subscriptions, but HSBC has not announced those functions as live at launch.

The current rollout is thus bounded by PayMe’s app and its supported payment routes. HSBC has not reported how many users have activated wallets, and the 3.3 million figure is the eligible user base, not confirmed RedCoin adoption. The launch announcement also leaves the on-chain architecture and external transfer support unspecified. Those details will determine whether RedCoin remains an app-contained payment option or becomes usable across a wider digital asset network.
