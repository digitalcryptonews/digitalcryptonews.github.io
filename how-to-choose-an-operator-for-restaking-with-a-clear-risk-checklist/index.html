How to Choose an Operator for Restaking With a Clear Risk Checklist

How to choose an operator for restaking: compare AVS duties, stake allocations, security evidence and fees before delegating ETH or liquid staking tokens.

The Finality DeskNov 27, 2026 · 06:49 UTC4 min read#29ac7f

Choose an operator whose AVS duties, stake allocations, operating record and delegation terms you can verify and accept. The operator runs services for AVSs, and your delegated stake can back those services and face their slashing conditions.

Say Maya has restaked a liquid staking token and is comparing two operators. One supports a narrower set of services and explains its allocations; the other supports more services but gives less detail about changes. The second may advertise higher rewards, but Maya should first work out what those services require and which stake could be exposed.

This is the same choice involved in delegated staking without running a node: on eigen layer, a restaker delegates to an operator, whose AVS participation shapes the stake’s exposure. The restaker chooses the operator, not individual AVSs directly.

Start with the operator’s AVS duties and allocations

An operator runs services for AVSs, while a restaker delegates stake to that operator. An AVS, or actively validated service, is a service secured by restaked assets. Delegation links your stake to an operator’s work; the operator’s service choices determine which duties and slashing conditions may apply.

Open the operator’s EigenLayer profile and follow its links to service documentation and status updates. List the AVSs and operator sets it supports. An operator set groups an operator’s participation for a service; read the service’s duties and slashing conditions, then check which stake the operator allocates to each set.

EigenLayer’s DelegationManager records the delegation. It covers the wallet’s deposited shares, and a restaker delegates to one operator at a time. The AllocationManager tracks operator stake allocation to operator sets and enables AVSs to slash allocated stake. So delegation alone does not tell you the full exposure: the operator’s allocations matter too.

Those allocations can change as the operator changes its participation. Look for a public update history and a clear way to learn about changes. If you cannot tell which AVSs and stake allocations your delegation may support, choose an operator with clearer disclosures or wait.

On eigen layer, the practical comparison is between the operator’s current service mix and its allocation choices, not just its name or advertised reward. Exposure follows the operator sets and stake allocations it supports, including later changes. Read the linked service terms again when those choices change.

Compare evidence and terms before you delegate

Compare an operator’s record, infrastructure and security practices against the duties of its AVSs. Look for public incident disclosures, operational updates and explanations of how it handles the specific work its services require. A general claim about reliability is less useful than evidence tied to those duties.

Then compare reward and fee terms. Confirm what is stated about rewards, fees and any conditions that affect them; do not infer a likely return from an advertised figure alone. A larger service mix may mean more varied duties and slashing conditions to understand. A narrower mix may be easier to assess, but its risks still depend on its actual AVSs and allocations.

For Maya, the operator with fewer services is the better fit only if she can verify its duties, allocation choices, operating evidence and terms—and accepts the resulting exposure. She should not select it simply because its service list is shorter. Before delegating, she compares both operators on the same criteria and chooses the one whose current arrangements she understands.

Before acting, confirm which asset or strategy you have restaked and that you accept the possibility of AVS slashing. Delegate only after you can identify the operator’s supported AVSs and operator sets, read their duties and slashing conditions, review allocations and changes, and accept the reward or fee terms. After delegation, keep monitoring the operator’s updates because its participation and your exposure may change.

Related

What does an EigenLayer operator do?

An EigenLayer operator runs services for AVSs and may allocate stake to the operator sets associated with those services. Restakers delegate to the operator, so its service duties and allocation choices shape their potential exposure.

Can I change my EigenLayer operator after delegating?

A restaker delegates to one operator at a time, so changing operators means changing that delegation. Before making a change, review the current operator’s arrangements and the new operator’s AVSs, allocations, slashing conditions and terms.
