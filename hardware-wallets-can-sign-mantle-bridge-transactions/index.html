Hardware Wallets Can Sign Mantle Bridge Transactions

A hardware wallet can authorize Mantle bridge calls through an EVM wallet interface, but deposits, token approvals and Ethereum claims may each need separate signatures.

The Finality DeskOct 16, 2026 · 11:35 UTC5 min read#4ae6c7

Yes: a hardware wallet can sign Mantle bridge transactions when it is connected to the bridge through a wallet interface that exposes its EVM account. The device signs each transaction with its private key; the bridge interface constructs and submits the call. This does not make one signature cover the whole transfer. An ERC-20 approval, a deposit and a later withdrawal claim are distinct transactions, each with its own network, fee and confirmation.

The longer guide to the mantle bridge covers route choice and transfer stages; the signing question turns on which account approves each on-chain call. The hardware wallet protects that account’s key, while the connected wallet software provides the bridge with an interface to request signatures. A device does not need a separate “bridge key” to authorize an EVM transaction.

Can a hardware wallet sign Mantle bridge calls?

Yes, if the wallet connector passes the transaction to an EVM account controlled by the device. For example, MetaMask can connect supported hardware wallets and request that they sign transactions. The address exposed through that connection must be the account holding the source-chain assets. Connecting a software wallet account in the same interface does not make that account hardware-backed.

For a deposit from Ethereum, the signer approves a transaction on Ethereum. For a withdrawal initiated on Mantle, the signer approves a transaction on Mantle. The device signs the transaction payload for the selected chain; it does not move tokens between networks by itself. Bridge contracts and the network’s cross-chain messaging process handle the transfer after the signed transaction is submitted.

This distinction matters when a wallet shows a transaction request. A successful signature authorizes that specific call. It does not guarantee that the destination transfer has completed, that the receiving application accepts the token representation, or that a later claim has been sent.

What does the wallet sign when depositing?

An ERC-20 deposit can require two Ethereum transactions. First, the token contract’s approve function grants the bridge contract an allowance to spend the selected token amount. Then the wallet signs the bridge deposit call. The approval only sets that allowance; it does not deposit the tokens. ETH deposits do not use an ERC-20 allowance, though the Ethereum transaction still needs ETH for gas.

The bridge call is the instruction that starts the cross-chain transfer. Once it is included on Ethereum, the bridge’s messaging path processes the deposit and makes the corresponding asset available on Mantle. The hardware wallet is responsible for authorizing the source-chain call. It does not sign a second transaction merely because the destination balance is updated.

For a token approval, check the spender and allowance shown by the wallet. An allowance larger than the intended deposit gives the spender permission to use more of that token later. If the device or wallet cannot show enough detail to establish what the call authorizes, do not approve it based only on the bridge page’s summary.

Why can a withdrawal need another signature?

A withdrawal starts with a transaction on Mantle, where the wallet authorizes the bridge call that records the exit. The transfer then passes through the bridge’s settlement process. When the exit is ready to complete on Ethereum, the wallet must sign a separate Ethereum claim transaction. That claim uses Ethereum gas and needs an available ETH balance, even when the asset being withdrawn is itself ETH.

The two signatures authorize calls on different networks. The first starts the withdrawal on Mantle; the second releases the funds on Ethereum after the bridge marks the exit ready. Closing the browser after the first signature does not undo the submitted withdrawal. Reconnecting the same account to the bridge history can show its status and, when available, the claim step.

Before signing, confirm the account and network in the wallet, then compare the destination address, token, amount and fee with the bridge request. Keep gas available on the chain where each transaction will be sent. The hardware device protects the key from being copied into the browser, but it cannot make a malicious contract call safe if the signer approves it.

What should you verify on the device?

Review the transaction the device actually receives, not only the bridge interface’s description. Hardware wallet displays vary: some decode a contract call into readable fields, while others show limited data or require blind signing. If the call is not legible enough to verify its target and purpose, the device cannot independently confirm that the interface prepared the intended bridge action.

  • Confirm the connected address is the hardware-backed account that holds the source asset.
  • Check that the wallet is on Ethereum for a deposit or final claim, and on Mantle for a withdrawal initiation.
  • Inspect the token approval’s spender and amount before signing it.
  • Keep the withdrawal initiation and the later Ethereum claim as separate pending steps in your plan.

The practical answer is yes, with a wallet connector that supports the device’s EVM account. The hardware wallet signs the transaction requests; the bridge contract and cross-chain process perform the transfer. Treat each approval, deposit, withdrawal initiation and claim as its own authorization, because each has a different effect and may require a different network’s gas.
