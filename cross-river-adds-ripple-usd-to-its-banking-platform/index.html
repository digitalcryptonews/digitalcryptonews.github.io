Cross River Adds Ripple USD to Its Banking Platform

Cross River is adding Ripple USD on the XRP Ledger to its banking platform, giving fintechs one API for stablecoin settlement alongside ACH, wires and real-time payments.

The Finality DeskOct 16, 2026 · 00:15 UTC3 min read#d2a853

Cross River Bank is integrating Ripple USD (RLUSD) on the XRP Ledger into its Core Operating System, giving fintech customers a single API connection for stablecoin payments alongside bank payment rails. The bank said on October 15 that the integration will support real-time settlement, cross-border payments and treasury transfers. It adds RLUSD as a payment rail within the banking platform; the announcement says services are currently available only in limited states. Cross River’s announcement describes the move as an expansion of its existing stablecoin infrastructure.

How will fintechs access RLUSD through Cross River?

Customers will access RLUSD through Cross River’s existing platform and API rather than deploying separate blockchain infrastructure, according to the bank. Cross River says the connection will let fintechs move between US dollars and RLUSD, make cross-border payments and treasury transfers around the clock, and access ACH, wires and real-time payments through one banking platform.

The announcement does not detail the API endpoints, supported transaction flows or how customers’ eligibility varies by state. It says RLUSD is a US dollar-backed stablecoin issued on the XRP Ledger, a public blockchain. Cross River presents the integration as a way to bring blockchain payments into its regulated banking platform, where businesses already access traditional payment rails.

Where does the XRP Ledger fit into settlement?

RLUSD transactions use the XRP Ledger for onchain settlement, while Cross River’s Core Operating System connects that activity to its banking services. The bank says XRP Ledger transactions settle in three to five seconds, can move 24/7 and cost a fraction of a cent. That describes the network’s transaction settlement; it does not mean every connected bank transfer or conversion completes on that timetable.

The companies say the combined setup is intended to give payment networks another settlement option, including for intraday settlement, and reduce settlement latency. Those are stated aims, not reported performance results for the new integration. The announcement provides no launch volumes, transaction limits or measured savings.

How does stablecoin activity appear in a bank account?

Cross River’s stablecoin payments product description explains its general accounting model: incoming stablecoin payments are received and recorded as fiat, while payouts convert from fiat. The page also says funds move only after compliance clears, describing a “good-funds” model that removes the need to prefund. The October announcement does not specify whether every detail of that product flow applies identically to RLUSD transactions.

That distinction matters for treasury and reconciliation. Onchain settlement can run continuously, while the bank-facing account activity is denominated and recorded in US dollars. Cross River says its platform integrates stablecoins with ACH, wires and real-time payments, allowing businesses to use the available rail for each payment flow. The release does not identify which payment networks or customer use cases will be enabled first.
