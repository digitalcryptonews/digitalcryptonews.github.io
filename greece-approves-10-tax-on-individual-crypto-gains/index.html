Greece approves 10% tax on individual crypto gains

Greece's parliament approved a 10% tax on individual crypto gains, with a €500 annual exemption; calculation and audit rules will follow in ministerial directives.

The Finality DeskNov 6, 2026 · 02:24 UTC3 min read#853c31

Greece’s parliament approved a 10% capital gains tax on cryptocurrency on Nov. 5, establishing the country’s first comprehensive framework for taxing crypto assets, Reuters reported. The bill exempts annual crypto gains of up to €500. A senior finance ministry official told Reuters that further directives will set transparency and audit rules.

The vote moves crypto gains into a defined tax regime after a gap in Greek law. The tax applies to individuals’ gains from transferring crypto assets. The ministry’s published breakdown of the bill describes how the taxable gain is calculated and how crypto-to-crypto swaps are treated.

How will Greece calculate a taxable crypto gain?

The ministry says the general calculation is the difference between an asset’s acquisition price and its transfer price. Successive purchases require rules for determining average acquisition cost, alongside documentation of transactions. Those rules matter when an investor acquired the same asset at different prices and later transfers only part of the holdings.

The annual exemption applies to gains up to €500 per tax year. Reuters reported the exemption as part of the approved bill. The ministry’s account says exchanging one crypto asset for another does not create a taxable gain. That treatment makes a swap distinct from a transfer that realizes a gain under the framework.

The ministry’s public summary was issued during the bill’s consultation period. Reuters reported that implementation directives are still to come and will define transparency and audit requirements. The available reports do not specify the complete documentation process or how the tax authority will handle records across multiple exchanges and wallets.

Does the 10% rate cover staking and lending returns?

The 10% rate applies to capital gains from transfers, according to Reuters. The ministry’s bill summary described returns from crypto lending, liquidity provision and staking as interest income taxed at 15%. Those returns therefore sit in a separate category from gains on disposal, despite arising from crypto activity.

The ministry summary also described an option to voluntarily declare gains from earlier transfers within 12 months of the law’s publication, without fines or interest, subject to conditions. It said crypto sales would not incur Greece’s Digital Transaction Fee. Reuters’ report confirms the vote and the capital gains tax, but does not detail these provisions. Their operation will depend on the final law and later guidance.

Why will the tax be difficult to measure?

The finance ministry official told Reuters that the state could not yet project revenue. The vast majority of Greek investors use platforms based abroad, the official said, making the domestic crypto market difficult to measure. The location of trading platforms creates a reporting and audit challenge for a tax based on individual gains and acquisition records.

EU countries do not have a unified crypto tax system, Reuters reported. Greece’s approved framework sets a domestic rate and exemption, while the ministry says additional directives will spell out controls. Until those rules are published, the reported framework establishes the tax treatment but leaves practical audit procedures to follow.
