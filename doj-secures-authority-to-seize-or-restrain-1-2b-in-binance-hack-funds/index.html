DOJ secures authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of a wider freeze as investigators trace laundering routes.

The Finality DeskNov 23, 2026 · 03:48 UTC3 min read#f352c4

The U.S. Justice Department says a court authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, part of a broader effort to stop funds from moving through the crypto ecosystem. The Justice Department announcement says the department and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far.

The department said the theft occurred on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The FBI refers to the North Korean military actors it blames as TraderTraitor. The latest announcement does not say that all $1.2 billion has been transferred into government custody: it describes court-authorized seizure or restraint of that amount within the larger pool of frozen funds.

That distinction matters. A freeze blocks assets from moving while investigators pursue recovery; seizure and forfeiture involve legal steps to take property and determine its disposition. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not detail the status of individual assets or the timing of any forfeiture proceedings.

The operation depends on tracing the stolen assets and working with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict them. The department said law enforcement worked closely with those providers after the theft. In practice, that coordination can prevent funds from being exchanged or withdrawn through services that can act on an account or asset under their control. The announcement does not identify the providers or describe the specific technical steps used to freeze each asset.

Investigators say the tracing effort remains active. TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, according to the Justice Department. Those routes complicate recovery by moving funds between networks and services, but the department did not identify particular bridges or mixers used in this case. It said the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery work.

The figures describe overlapping stages of the response, not separate recoveries to add together. The more than $2.7 billion figure covers assets frozen through combined U.S. government and private-sector efforts; the more than $1.2 billion figure is the portion for which the department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain. The announcement does not say how much has been returned to Binance or its users.

For now, the legal authorization changes what the government can seek to do with a significant part of the frozen pool. It does not end the tracing process or establish the final ownership of recovered assets. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen cryptocurrency are ongoing as investigators follow the laundering routes.
