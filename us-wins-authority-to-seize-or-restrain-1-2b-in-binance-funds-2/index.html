US wins authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance funds

US authorities say court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen as laundering continues across crypto services.

The Finality DeskNov 19, 2026 · 16:04 UTC3 min read#890572

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said on Nov. 12. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion that government agencies and private-sector partners say they have frozen since the theft. The Justice Department’s announcement describes a legal and operational process still under way, rather than a completed recovery of the full sum.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor. The department says the attack occurred on or about Oct. 29 and took more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The Associated Press reported the loss at about $6.3 billion. The FBI had publicly attributed the attack to North Korea before the seizure announcement, which added the cumulative freeze and court-authorization figures.

Those figures describe different stages. Partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total; within that pool, the Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. A freeze can stop assets from moving through a service that controls access to them, while a court order authorizes legal restraint or seizure. The announcement does not say that the government has already taken custody of every asset covered by the order, or that the entire $2.7 billion is subject to it.

The response depends on coordination with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. The Justice Department says federal divisions and law-enforcement partners worked with those providers to interdict funds. The FBI’s cyber alert on the Binance attack urges exchanges, DeFi services, instant exchanges, THORChain node operators and other providers to block transactions from or derived from listed addresses. That makes the provider layer a point of intervention as funds move between public-chain addresses and services capable of withholding transfers.

The tracing problem is ongoing. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks; mixers and exchange services can add intermediaries between the original theft and later transfers. Each hop can create new addresses and service relationships for investigators and providers to trace. The FBI says it has observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expects further conversions through DeFi and instant-swap services.

The FBI’s alert identifies addresses it says hold or have held assets from the theft and are operated by, or closely connected to, TraderTraitor. It asks service operators to block transactions linked to those addresses. That request is not a consensus rule that reverses or invalidates transfers on a blockchain. The announcement instead describes freezes and legal restraints applied through cooperation with providers, alongside investigations to trace funds across chains and services.

Forfeiture remains a separate legal step. The AP reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim and a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets continue. It did not specify the assets or chains covered by the $1.2 billion authorization, or give a timetable for returning any recovered funds. The figures mark a major interruption to the laundering path, but they do not establish how much will ultimately be forfeited or returned.
